The thesis

The best hotel asset in India is the one nobody wants to photograph.

Leisure hospitality competes for a guest who might not come. Industrial hospitality serves a guest whose employer has already signed the contract that puts them there.

The leisure hotel

  • Guests choose to come, and can choose not to
  • Occupancy collapses for four months a year
  • 18–22% of every booking goes to an OTA
  • Competes with every other hotel in the city
  • Weekend-weighted, holiday-dependent, weather-exposed
  • Rate is high in December and irrelevant in July

The Mevad hotel

  • Guests are sent, on a schedule set by capital expenditure
  • Occupancy holds within a narrow band all twelve months
  • Booked direct, on annual corporate rate agreements
  • Competes with a ninety-minute drive and an unbranded lodge
  • Weekday-weighted, shift-driven, weather-indifferent
  • Rate is modest - and it is the same every month

Lower ADR. Higher, steadier RevPAR. That trade is the entire proposition, and it is the one most investors get backwards. A Mevad room will never command what a beachfront suite commands in December. It will also never sit empty in July.

You are not buying the highest point on the curve. You are buying the shape of the curve - and over a ten-year hold, shape beats peak.

The asset

Built for the guest who arrives at 2am.

Engineered around a person who works a twelve-hour shift and stays for months. Every design decision follows from that, and none of them follow from a brochure.

Property specification
Standard across the chain.

A city hotel treats a long-stay guest as an anomaly. A Mevad hotel is built for them: forty per cent of keys are long-stay inventory, the kitchen runs to shift timings rather than restaurant timings, and the laundry is sized for a guest who packed three shirts for a six-month posting.

None of it photographs well. All of it is why the anchor plant next door signs an annual rate agreement instead of putting people in a lodge.

The chain

One hotel open. Four more corridors underwritten.

Each property is underwritten against the industrial base around it - the count of anchor employers, the sector mix, and the distance to the gate.

Model it yourself

Run your own numbers - including the ones we'd rather you didn't.

Every assumption behind these figures is exposed and adjustable. Drag occupancy down to 40% and watch what happens. We would rather you found the floor here than after you had signed.

Why ownership, not a fixed return. There is no assured or guaranteed leg here. You own a percentage of a real property - or, once it opens, of the parent company - and you are paid your share of what it actually earns, after a flat 20% management fee. That is the whole model.

Why the first payout is not year one. A property under construction cannot pay a return, and one that just opened does not reach stabilised occupancy for a further nine to twelve months. The model applies both. Projections that skip this are the reason investors are disappointed.
